Manipulative Information and Media

Interactive media lets users click, choose, answer, move, or control content. Think of it as a conversation with media: you do something, the system responds, and you decide what to do next.

Essential question: How can interactive media help audiences participate, respond, and learn, not just click for the sake of clicking?

Core idea: passive vs. manipulative media

The key difference is whether the audience can influence what happens next. Ask: can the user click, type, vote, choose, drag, submit, zoom, or explore?

Passive media

The audience mainly receives the message. Examples: printed poster, regular photo, prerecorded lecture, radio broadcast.

Manipulative / interactive media

The audience performs an action and the media changes, responds, records, or reveals something. Examples: poll, form, quiz, interactive map, simulation.

Different interactive tools are designed for different goals. Pick a type to see its purpose.

Where students encounter it

School

LMS quiz, attendance form, class poll, interactive module

Social media

Story polls, reaction buttons, Q&A stickers, live chat

Community

Online appointment form, public-service portal, interactive map

Shopping / services

Filters, booking calendars, order customization, ratings

Interactivity is not limited to games. It appears anywhere the user can influence the content or process.

What makes interactive media effective

Good interactivity is easy to use and connected to a clear purpose.

Tap a characteristic to see what it means.
"Engaging" does not mean "full of effects." It means the interaction helps the audience think or act.

Design principles

Contrast

Make important buttons and questions stand out.
Bad: gray button on gray background.
Better: clear visual difference.

Alignment

Keep text, choices, and buttons organized.
Bad: choices scattered randomly.
Better: choices lined up.

Repetition

Use consistent colors, fonts, and labels.
Bad: every screen looks different.
Better: same button style.

Proximity

Place related instructions and controls together.
Bad: directions far from question.
Better: directions beside task.

Also check readability and simple navigation.

Planning a webinar interaction

Before the discussion

Use a poll to check prior knowledge. "Which source do you trust most for emergency information?" Purpose: reveal assumptions before teaching.

During the discussion

Use a quick quiz or chat prompt. "Which design principle is missing in this slide?" Purpose: check understanding while teaching.

After the discussion

Use a reflection form or Q&A. "What will you change in your own media output?" Purpose: connect learning to action.

The same tool can serve different purposes depending on when and why it is used.

Five-step planning process

  1. Choose. Pick poll, quiz, Q&A, form, or reflection.
  2. Write. Create one clear instruction and focused question.
  3. Check. Review relevance, clarity, usability, accessibility, and privacy.
  4. Test. Open the link or feature before presenting.
  5. Use results. Discuss what the responses mean. Do not just collect them.

Guided practice: choose the best tool

Lesson synthesis

Manipulative media

lets users control, respond, choose, or explore.

Good interaction

has a clear purpose and gives useful feedback.

Good selection

checks relevance, clarity, accuracy, usability, accessibility, and privacy.

Good design

makes the next action easy to see and understand.
